Background

I'm in my final year of a BASc at the University of Otago, double-majoring in Computer Science and Information Science. Before that, I was dux of Southland Boys' High School and picked up a handful of academic scholarships along the way — useful at the time, but it's the years since that have actually shaped what I build.

Fonterra

Three internships on Fonterra's Automation Engineering team gave me a strange parallel track: HMI development in AVEVA InTouch, standardising PLC tag naming across sites, and building a desktop GUI for tuning PID controllers on a dairy manufacturing line. None of it is glamorous, but it taught me how software behaves when it has to run unattended on a factory floor for years, not just survive a demo.

Music, then software

Music is the longer-running interest — I've been producing for years, releasing under the name MSG. The plugins started the way most of my software does: I wanted a tool that didn't exist, so I learned enough C++ and JUCE to build it myself. KickDucker came out of that. Preverb is the one I'm building now.
